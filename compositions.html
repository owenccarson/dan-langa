Duhkha

2020 | 10 minutes
for string quartet

Program Note

The Four Noble Truths of Buddhism are the backbone of the Buddha's teachings. They are the truth of suffering, the truth of the cause of suffering, the truth of the end of suffering, and the truth of the path that leads to the end of suffering. The First Noble Truth, Duhkha, acknowledges the presence of suffering in the world.

Winner of the 2020 Du Vert À L'infini Festival Competition. Performed by the Scheherazade Quartet. Recorded at Persian Sound City, Tehran.

BEAM

2019 (rev. 2025) | 10 minutes
for percussion quartet

Program Note

BEAM is an exploration of intervals and their coloring of sound. Using three major triads (A, E, and B), the piece oscillates between bright fifths and dark fourths. It weaves through different blends of these chords layered on top of one another, forming an ever-changing cloud.

SOLO

Lines, Not Straight, Not Touching

2020 | 5 minutes
for solo cello

Program Note

'Lines, Not Straight, Not Touching' gets its title from the 1971 Sol LeWitt etching of the same name. On a cream-colored canvas, LeWitt places a cascading series of lines in four colors contained within a neat box. As you move closer to the etching, the seemingly chaotic image takes on a new character and each individual line reveals its intricacies. At close proximity, the space between the lines becomes more clear and the careful dance each line displays on the page can be admired.

Refraction

2017 | 3 minutes
for solo piano

Program Note

This piece creates a transition between Gÿgory Ligeti’s “Étude 5: Arc-en-ciel” from Etudes Pour Piano and Issac Albéniz’s “Evocacion” from Iberia - Book 1. Sitting with these two pieces, I found three or four impactful moments within each work. The shared characteristic of each of these moments was the feeling of harmonic stasis. The composers allowed the listener to bask in a sonority used in the context of otherwise restless harmonic palettes. I sat with these select portions of each of the pieces frustrated in how sudden and jagged it felt when I tried to transition between the Ligeti and the Albéniz material. I was trying too hard to find similarities within the given material and forcing them together to be one piece.
